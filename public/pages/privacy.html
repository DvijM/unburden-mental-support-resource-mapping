Privacy Policy & Data Governance

Unburden is an independent educational mental health support and resource-mapping platform developed in alignment with UN Sustainable Development Goal 3.4. This document explains honestly and clearly how your information is handled.

1. What Information We Collect

  • Account Information (Mandatory for registered accounts): Name, email address, and a salted scrypt password hash. Country, age, and phone number are optional.
  • Self-Assessment Records (Opt-In Only): PHQ-9, GAD-7, and WHO-5 question responses, total score, severity band, and functional impact rating — stored in the database only if you explicitly grant Assessment Result Storage consent.
  • Mood Check-Ins (Opt-In Only): Voluntary 1–5 mood ratings and brief reflection notes — stored only if you grant Mood Tracking consent.
  • Platform Usage Counters: Anonymous, session-deduplicated counters of soundtrack plays and educational article views used to rank helpful resources.

2. Why Information Is Collected

We process data solely to provide self-guided screening feedback, display country-relevant crisis helplines, allow you to review your longitudinal wellness history, and — only when you explicitly request it — share selected records with a Verified Responder you nominate.

3. Which Features Work Without an Account

You can browse the Global Helpline Directory (/find-support), read educational articles (/resources), listen to ambient soundtracks (/sounds), and view immediate PHQ-9 self-check feedback in your browser without creating an account.

4. Granular Consent & How to Withdraw It

Account creation consent is never bundled with optional health data storage or sharing. From your Privacy & Wellness Dashboard, you can independently grant or revoke consent for:

  • assessment_storage — Saving PHQ-9 / GAD-7 / WHO-5 results to your account history.
  • mood_tracking — Saving 1–5 mood check-ins.
  • activity_history — Saving personal resource and soundtrack history.
  • responder_sharing — Allowing nominated Responders to view selected categories.
  • research_analytics — Contributing de-identified severity bands to aggregate statistics.

5. Who Can Access Your Data & Audit Logging

Super Administrators do not have automatic access to your individual mental health assessments or mood records. Administrators only see anonymized, aggregate platform statistics and account status metadata.

Individual assessment or mood records can only be viewed by a verified Responder whom you have explicitly nominated via a time-limited Share Grant. Every time a Responder views your shared record, an immutable entry is written to your personal Access Audit Log in your dashboard.

6. Data Export & Permanent Erasure ("Delete My Data")

At any time, you may download a complete machine-readable JSON archive of your profile, assessments, mood check-ins, consents, share grants, and access audit logs via Export My Data on your dashboard.

Selecting Permanently Delete My Data immediately deletes your user account, active sessions, saved assessments, mood check-ins, consent history, activity events, and responder share grants from the active MongoDB database, and anonymizes personal references in security audit logs.

7. Retention & Backup Purge Cycle

Active records are retained until you delete them or delete your account. Sessions expire automatically via database TTL indexes. When you delete your account, records are removed immediately from the primary database; encrypted infrastructure backups managed by our cloud database provider are automatically overwritten on a rolling 30-day retention cycle.

8. Security Architecture & Honest Scope

Unburden uses HTTPS transport encryption, salted scrypt password hashing, HTTP-only session cookies with CSRF protection, strict server-side role-based access control (RBAC), and MongoDB schema validation. Unburden is an educational screening and resource-mapping platform, not a hospital electronic medical record system or emergency dispatch provider.

9. Emergency Limitations

Unburden does not monitor self-assessments in real time and cannot dispatch emergency services. If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services (such as 112, 911, or 999) or connect directly with a crisis helpline on our Find Support page.
